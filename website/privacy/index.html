Privacy Policy

Vybecord browser extension and desktop app · Last updated 30 October 2026

The short version. The extension reads what is playing in your music tabs and sends it to the Vybecord app running on your own computer, at 127.0.0.1. It has no server, no account, and no analytics.

The desktop app makes one exception, and only one. Discord can only show a cover that has a web address, so when your player hands the app its cover as an image — Apple Music, a local file, a desktop player — the app publishes that one image so Discord shows exactly what your player shows. Never the audio, and nothing that identifies you. It is described in full below, and it has a switch.

The extension can also, if you turn it on, lend the app your YouTube sign-in for age-restricted captions. That switch is off unless you flip it; it is described below too.

What the extension reads

On the six sites it supports — Spotify, YouTube and YouTube Music, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, Twitch and Kick — it reads the metadata of what is currently playing:

  • Track, video, or stream title
  • Artist, uploader, or channel name
  • Album or release name, where the site shows one
  • Cover or thumbnail image address
  • Playback position, duration, and whether playback is paused
  • The address of the item playing, so the app can link to it

It reads this from the page you already have open. It does not sign in on your behalf, and out of the box it has no access to your account on any of these services. The one exception is a switch you have to turn on yourself, described next.

Your YouTube sign-in, only if you turn it on

YouTube gives the captions of an age-restricted video only to a signed-in, age-verified account, and the app cannot sign in. The extension's options have a switch, Share my YouTube sign-in for captions, which is off unless you turn it on. Turning it on makes your browser ask you for two extra permissions: reading cookies, and access to youtube.com. Until you grant them, the extension cannot read a single cookie.

  • Only when asked. The extension hands anything over only after the app reports that a video you are watching came back age-restricted, and at most once every 30 seconds.
  • Only YouTube's cookies, and only to the app at 127.0.0.1. Cookies of any other site are never read, and nothing goes to any server of ours.
  • Kept in memory, briefly. The app keeps them for ten minutes, never on disk except inside the temporary folder of the one caption request that uses them, and uses them only to fetch that age-restricted video's captions from YouTube. Every other video is still fetched signed out.
  • Turning the switch off gives the permissions back to the browser, and nothing is sent again.

That request to YouTube is YouTube's: it is covered by Google's privacy policy.

Where that data goes

To one place: http://127.0.0.1:8888 — the Vybecord desktop app, on the machine you are sitting at. 127.0.0.1 is the address a computer uses to talk to itself; traffic to it never reaches a network. It is the only address the extension's manifest permits it to contact, and the only place what you are playing is ever sent.

One kind of request goes elsewhere, and it carries none of that. On kick.com, the extension asks Kick's own API about the page you are on: the channel's name, picture, stream title, category, whether it is live and when the stream started, or the same details for a past broadcast or a clip. The page does not show all of it in a form that can be read reliably. That request goes to the site you are already looking at, is the kind the Kick page makes for itself, and contains only the channel name, video or clip identifier already in your address bar. Kick is the only site this happens on; everywhere else the extension reads the page and asks no one anything.

For Twitch, the desktop app (not the extension) asks Twitch's public API for the same details about the channel, past broadcast or clip in your Twitch tab. That request carries only the channel name or the video or clip identifier, and no sign-in.

The desktop app then publishes what you are playing to your Discord profile, which is the purpose of installing it. That step is Discord's, and is covered by Discord's privacy policy. If the app is not running, pushes from the extension simply fail and nothing happens.

Cover art the desktop app publishes

This section is about the desktop app, not the extension. The extension uploads nothing, anywhere, ever.

Discord displays artwork by address, not by file. When the cover already has an address (Spotify in the browser, YouTube, SoundCloud), that address is used and nothing is uploaded.

Some players hand the app their cover as an image instead: Apple Music, the Spotify desktop app through Windows, a local file, other desktop players. There is no address for Discord to load, so the app publishes that single image to a store we run at vybecord-art.vybecord.workers.dev, hosted on Cloudflare, and hands Discord the resulting address. That way your profile shows exactly the cover your player shows, not another edition's.

Only when there is no such image, or when you turn publishing off, does the app look the song up on a public music catalogue — Deezer, then Apple. That sends only the track and artist name, and nothing of yours is uploaded.

Precisely what that involves:

  • The cover image, and nothing else. Not the audio file, not its name, not its folder, not its tags, and nothing about the rest of your library.
  • Metadata is stripped before it leaves your machine. EXIF, XMP and embedded comments are removed — camera model, timestamps and GPS coordinates among them. Only the picture itself is sent.
  • The address is the image. A cover is stored under the SHA‑256 hash of its own bytes. That address carries no username, no account, no device identifier and no timestamp. Two people who own the same cover produce the same address and share one stored file.
  • Nothing is recorded alongside it. The store holds images. It has no accounts, keeps no log tying an upload to a person, and cannot link two uploads to each other.
  • Stored covers are publicly readable by anyone holding the address — that is what lets Discord fetch them. An address cannot be guessed, but it is not a secret either: anyone who already has the identical image file can compute the same address. Treat this as publishing the picture, because it is.
  • It is a switch, not a fact of life. Settings → Integrations → Cover images → Publish the player’s cover. Turned off, nothing is ever uploaded: covers come from a catalogue, and tracks that are in none fall back to the default placeholder. You can also point the app at your own store instead of ours.

What is stored, and where

  • Your per-site switches (which of the six sites are enabled) are kept in the browser's synced extension storage. If you are signed into your browser, it syncs them across your devices the same way it syncs your bookmarks. They are six true/false values and nothing else.
  • A stream start time, with the channel or video it belongs to, is kept in that site's own local storage, under keys beginning with vybecord:, so a live stream's elapsed counter survives a page reload.

Uninstalling the extension removes the switches. The stream start time stays in that site's storage until you clear the site's data in your browser.

What is never collected

  • No browsing history, and no awareness of any tab other than the supported sites
  • No passwords, form data, or payment information
  • No cookies, except YouTube's, and only if you turn on the switch described above
  • No name, email address, or account identifier
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, or advertising identifiers
  • No data sold or rented, by the extension or the app. The cover store above is the only service we operate: it holds images, has no accounts, and receives nothing else

Permissions, and why each is needed

  • Access to the six supported sites — to read the metadata of what is playing. Without it there is nothing to report.
  • Access to http://127.0.0.1:8888 — to reach the desktop app. This is a local address, not a website.
  • storage — to remember which sites you switched off.
  • Optional: cookies and access to youtube.com — asked for only when you turn on Share my YouTube sign-in for captions, to read YouTube's cookies for age-restricted captions. Not granted otherwise. Firefox also asks your consent to share authentication information.

Your control

Every site has its own switch in the extension's options, and turning one off stops the reporting for that site immediately, without a page reload. The YouTube sign-in switch is in the same place. Closing the desktop app stops everything. Uninstalling the extension removes it and its stored settings.

Cover publishing has its own switch in the app, under Settings → Integrations → Cover images, and turning it off takes effect on the next track. If a cover of yours is already in the store and you want it gone, open an issue with its address and it will be deleted; because the address is a hash of the image, nothing else can be relying on it.
