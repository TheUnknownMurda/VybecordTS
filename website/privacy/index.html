Privacy Policy

Vybecord browser extension · Last updated 20 August 2026

The short version. The extension reads what is playing in your music tabs and sends it to the Vybecord app running on your own computer, at 127.0.0.1. It has no server, no account, and no analytics. Nothing is sent to us, because there is no “us” to send it to.

What the extension reads

On the six sites it supports — Spotify, YouTube and YouTube Music, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, Twitch and Kick — it reads the metadata of what is currently playing:

  • Track, video, or stream title
  • Artist, uploader, or channel name
  • Album or release name, where the site shows one
  • Cover or thumbnail image address
  • Playback position, duration, and whether playback is paused
  • The address of the item playing, so the app can link to it

It reads this from the page you already have open. It does not sign in on your behalf, and it has no access to your account on any of these services.

Where that data goes

To one place: http://127.0.0.1:8888 — the Vybecord desktop app, on the machine you are sitting at. 127.0.0.1 is the address a computer uses to talk to itself; traffic to it never reaches a network. The extension is technically incapable of sending anywhere else, because that address is the only one its manifest permits it to contact.

The desktop app then publishes what you are playing to your Discord profile, which is the purpose of installing it. That step is Discord's, and is covered by Discord's privacy policy. If the app is not running, pushes from the extension simply fail and nothing happens.

What is stored, and where

  • Your per-site switches (which of the six sites are enabled) are kept in the browser's synced extension storage. If you are signed into your browser, it syncs them across your devices the same way it syncs your bookmarks. They are six true/false values and nothing else.
  • A stream start time is kept in the page's local storage, under keys beginning with vybecord:, so a live stream's elapsed counter survives a page reload.

Uninstalling the extension removes both.

What is never collected

  • No browsing history, and no awareness of any tab other than the supported sites
  • No passwords, cookies, form data, or payment information
  • No name, email address, or account identifier
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, or advertising identifiers
  • No data sold, rented, or shared with third parties — there is no third party

Permissions, and why each is needed

  • Access to the six supported sites — to read the metadata of what is playing. Without it there is nothing to report.
  • Access to http://127.0.0.1:8888 — to reach the desktop app. This is a local address, not a website.
  • storage — to remember which sites you switched off.

Your control

Every site has its own switch in the extension's options, and turning one off stops the reporting for that site immediately, without a page reload. Closing the desktop app stops everything. Uninstalling the extension removes it and its stored settings.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the revision date above changes with it and the previous version stays in the site's public git history.

Questions, or something here that does not match what you observe? Open an issue at github.com/TheUnknownMurda/VybecordTS/issues. The extension is open source: you can read every line of what is described above.
